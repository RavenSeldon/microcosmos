# The Dark Microcosmos: A Classroom Guide

How to play, what happens, and why your choices matter. This follows the shape of Astra's guide
to the original game (`docs/design-history/10-classroom-guide-original-game.md`), rewritten for
the game as it is played now. The keeper’s notes and the worlds’ guidance teach these ideas inside the game; nobody needs
to read this guide first.

## 1. Meet Your World

Imagine six little towns around one shared pond. Each town uses water every day. Used water is
not gone: it can be made clean again, but each town can only clean part of what it uses.

There is a seventh community, living on the pond itself: the **Seventh Basin**. You are its
keeper. You cannot give any town orders. You can listen, ask, help from the basin's own water,
and wait.

| Thing | What it means |
|---|---|
| 6 worlds | Obsidian Dusk, Verdance, Ember Glass, Still Sanctum, Prism Reach, The Unobserved |
| 18 moments | One journey: 6 under an open sky, 6 in the dark (no rain), 6 as rain returns |
| 1 choice a moment | You cannot fix everything at once. Letting it flow is also a choice |
| Clean water | What a world drinks. Kept in its store, and in the basin |
| Used water | What is left after drinking. It waits in the world's holding until made clean |

## 2. What You Can Do

- **Listen** (free): choose a world. You see how it is, what you have learned about it, and
  under every choice, what that choice would change over the next three moments.
- **Open a current** between two worlds: they share spare water, and each can clean what the
  other has used. A world can say no, and each world has only so many ports.
- **Rest a current**: close it, and free the ports.
- **Tend a world from the basin**: send it clean water and carry some used water away.
- **Ask a world for what it does best**: each has one ability that lasts three moments.
- **Let it flow**: do nothing this moment. Often right.

## 3. The Worlds Are Different, and You Find Out How

Each world is good under a different sky. Verdance cleans a great deal under an open sky and
almost nothing in the dark. Ember Glass is the reverse. The game does not print this at the
start: the first time you see it happen, it is written under that world and stays there.

Nothing dangerous is ever hidden. A coming thirst, a cost, or a world about to take from another
is always shown before it happens.

## 4. Follow the Water

When water moves you can watch it: **gold** light is used water going to be cleaned, **blue**
is clean water being shared. The rule underneath is that water is never invented:

    water held before + rain + mist − the basin's own drinking − spills − losses = water held after

"The water" in the top bar shows these numbers for the last moment.

## 5. The Worlds Act Without You

Open currents keep working every moment without being asked again. And a world in trouble acts
for itself: if it goes thirsty, cannot cover its next need, and has no current to a world with
plenty, it announces that next moment it will take from that world's stores. You get one moment
to answer: ask the other to give freely, tend the thirsty one, open a current between them,
raise Obsidian's shelter, or let it happen.

## 6. What Goes Wrong

- **Strained:** a world's holding is nearly full of used water.
- **Spill:** a full holding overflows. Spilled water has left for good.
- **Thirsty:** a world could not drink what it needed this moment.
- **Dormant:** thirsty two moments running. The world sleeps until it holds water again.

## 7. How It Ends

| Ending | What it takes |
|---|---|
| A shared dawn | Every world awake, no thirst in the last three moments, no holding nearly full |
| A fragile peace | The journey completes with something unresolved; the ending says what |
| The long night | The worlds spent six moments asleep between them. The count shows from the first |

A hard moment in the dark does not rule out a shared dawn. Every moment can be undone.

## 8. Two Ways to Travel

The first screen offers the same journey two ways. **Let the worlds guide me** offers written
guidance from one world at a time, after the water has moved. **Find my own way** leaves that
guidance off. The rules, forecasts and possibilities are identical. The keeper’s notes switch
between these preferences; music has its own on/off control.

The speaking world is marked on the orbit. **Listen** opens its panel; **Heard** dismisses the
line. Both are free. You can also simply make your next choice. Auto-flow pauses when new guidance
appears so that reading never becomes a race.

## 9. One-Page Cheat Sheet

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who am I? | The keeper of the Seventh Basin |
| What do I do? | One choice a moment, or let it flow |
| How long do I have? | Eighteen moments |
| What is free? | Listening |
| What do currents do? | Share spare water; let one world clean what another used |
| Why would a world take from another? | It is thirsty, with no current to one that has plenty |
| Can I lose by doing nothing? | Yes: the long night falls at about moment 12 |
| Can one clever network win for me? | No: every arrangement needs at least one later adjustment |
| What is the best ending? | A shared dawn |

The question underneath is the one Astra named for the original game, and it still holds: can
you look after a whole world when you have very little power, cannot see everything at first,
and only eighteen chances to act?

**What this guide cannot tell you:** whether the game is fun. Nobody has played it yet.

## Playing Together

A first-time player can follow one world: listen, draw a current, and watch the water answer.
A returning player can ask what changes under the dark, when a current is worth resting, or
which kind of independence the orbit can support. Neither needs another rule set.

For shared play, take turns choosing a moment. Before committing, each person can say what they
expect; afterwards, open **Read this moment** to compare. There is no countdown. Undo lets the
group try a different answer. Read the voices aloud if a younger player wants help with the text.

Useful questions: Who had room to help? What became possible after that current opened? What
would you try differently under another sky? The game models particular water rules; it does
not prove a moral law about real people. Suitability and enjoyment across ages still need
playtesting with people of those ages.
