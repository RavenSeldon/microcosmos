## The approved rule

## STRIKE — Precise Interdiction

Proposed G1d rule, not yet tested

Immediate effect: Interrupt the target's network action for the current round. No water extraction disrupted, no artificial catch-up, no water destroyed.

Immediate cost: Increase station exposure by +3. The target may retaliate once in the same round under its existing policy.

Persistent cost: The grievance remains in the relationship history and may affect future cooperation.

Critical correction: An old grievance alone cannot trigger a new station attack every subsequent round. Independent threats, exposure-based attacks, and fresh provocations remain possible.

In implementation terms, the retaliatory opportunity belongs to the specific STRIKE event, not to a permanent `hasEverBeenStruck` condition.

If that opportunity is not exercised during the current round, it expires. Another STRIKE creates another opportunity.

Do not alter the existing SIGNAL acceptance rules yet. Permanent diplomatic reluctance and perpetual physical retaliation are different mechanisms, and isolating them makes the experiment interpretable.

## Why this is better gameplay

The distinction creates a much more compelling decision:

I can stop this attack. It might cost me an integrity point. Is the protection worth it?

That's an immediate, intelligible sacrifice.

The player still faces meaningful tradeoffs. Striking against a threat that would have lapsed wastes a turn and creates unnecessary hostility. Striking the correct aggressor can protect another district. Repeated aggression remains dangerous because each new strike carries exposure and possible retaliation.

Most importantly, the consequences can be understood without studying the simulation code.
