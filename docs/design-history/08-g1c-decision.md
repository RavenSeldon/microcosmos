# THE DARK MICROCOSMOS

G1 DECISION · GAMEPLAY BEFORE OPTIMIZATION

I accept both changes: switch to refined STRIKE, and make district welfare consequential to the game's outcome.

The comparison has clarified the problem. We don't need a more elaborate weapon. We need a game where protecting another world can actually matter.

There is one important distinction: the results show that precise interdiction is less physically harmful, not that it is more effective at winning. Its survival rate is 15.3%, compared with 43.7% for defusing threats through SIGNAL.

That gap is too large to ignore, but it also tells us where to focus.

In the tested scenarios, refined STRIKE protects districts more effectively but often loses the basin.

This is not necessarily a design failure. Protecting someone can entail a real sacrifice. The failure is that our current victory condition barely recognizes that sacrifice.

The danger is now making the opposite mistake: defining any act of protection as inherently victorious.

We should reward actual protection, not the player's intention.

## 2. Change the ending, not the number of buttons

I propose retaining three outcomes, with a clearer hierarchy.

Shared Dawn — the ideal outcome

Station intact, basin survives, and no district suffers serious unmet essential need over the six rounds.

Fragile Peace — survival with consequences

Station and basin survive, but some districts suffer serious hardship. The ending acknowledges the harm without declaring survival worthless.

Long Night — collapse

Station destroyed or basin lost.

For the first experiment, reuse the existing definition of serious unmet need: at least two units in a round. That is a provisional threshold, not a morally or empirically justified universal standard.

Do not require districts to finish with full reserves. Instead, show depleted reserves and outstanding obligations in the ending so the player knows whether the peace is sustainable.

Most importantly, Shared Dawn becomes the player's aspirational objective, not merely a decorative label after the existing win calculation.

That means a protective action can earn its turn by preventing distress, provided it doesn't cause the entire commons to collapse.

## 3. Refined STRIKE becomes INTERDICTION

Keep its visible name STRIKE.

STRIKE · TARGET E

OFFENSIVE INTERVENTION

Interrupt an imminent hostile action. The attacker loses its network action this round; the water supply is not attacked.

Costs: one turn, increased exposure, and potential grievance or retaliation.

The dramatic moment should be this:

District E threatens B. B has high need and almost no reserve. A conservation request would force an already desperate district to bear another sacrifice.

You have a choice.

SIGNAL B and ask it to find another way.

Or STRIKE E and stop the threatened attack, accepting the danger yourself.

That is the sort of offensive decision worth preserving in a compassionate dark forest.

It isn't compassion versus aggression. It is a choice about who bears the cost of protection.

And the rules should permit both choices to fail under different circumstances.

## 4. What to authorize now

## G1c — Outcome alignment

A bounded experiment, not another major redesign.

1. Switch the playable STRIKE setting to precise network interdiction. Preserve the original variant for reproducibility.
2. Make Shared Dawn a primary measured outcome, with serious district distress as a disqualifier. Keep Fragile Peace as a legitimate survival result.
3. Repeat the comparison on matched seeds, teaching the bots to recognize threats and distinguishing basin survival, protected districts, station survival, and ending quality.
4. Check full-knowledge necessity: identify whether some worlds require a protective strike to achieve Shared Dawn, even when SIGNAL is available.
5. Build the 2D playable immediately afterward, regardless of whether all four actions are perfectly balanced.

I would not adjust the basin clock yet. If Shared Dawn remains unattainable through protection because the basin fails too quickly, then we have grounds to test a longer clock. But we should not simultaneously change both systems and lose the ability to explain the result.

One more important measurement: compare the best conditional strategies, not merely a bot that strikes whenever threatened against one that always tries to cooperate. A good player should learn when to do either.

## 5. The #PLAY standard

We have now done substantial simulation work. It has caught real problems, and that was worthwhile.

But we are nearing the point where further optimization could become counterproductive.

I want four experiences in the first playable:

- LISTEN: I understand something I couldn't see before.
- SIGNAL: I changed another world's decision without forcing it.
- STRIKE: I prevented something consequential, and I accepted a risk to do it.
- HIDE: Sometimes restraint and survival are the smartest choices.

These should be felt through visible consequences, not explained by paragraphs of tutorial text.

There is no need for equal action frequencies or a perfectly balanced mathematical system. A rare but memorable STRIKE can be more satisfying than an action used every round.

And if the first human testers never find STRIKE useful, we should revise it based on their actual play.

My decision is to authorize refined STRIKE and outcome alignment as G1c, followed by the 2D playable. Retain the existing baseline and do not add REVEAL or SHIELD now.

The game should reward preventing harm while acknowledging that shared survival still matters. We must not solve one problem by making the basin irrelevant.

The design principle worth keeping is:

In the dark, strength is not the ability to destroy another world. It is the ability to protect one without destroying the rest.
