### THE DARK MICROCOSMOS

DESIGN SYNTHESIS v0.3 · COMPASSIONATE DARK FOREST

In a universe where every world is afraid of the others, can compassion become the strongest strategy?

I think we have found the game's defining idea.

The Dark Microcosmos should be a game in which cooperation is generally the most effective way to survive, but is never guaranteed, never effortless, and never morally imposed.

Violence remains possible. Defensive aggression can be rational. Trust can be misplaced. But the game's deeper discovery is that fear becomes costly when every little world depends on the others.

This is a stronger premise than simply asking whether to attack an unknown civilization.

The philosophical inspiration is particularly close to Indra's net in Huayan Buddhism: a network of jewels in which each reflects all the others, expressing mutual interdependence. Our adaptation is playful rather than doctrinal: six worlds cannot see into one another, yet their choices continually remake one another's circumstances.

Design principle: The others are neither enemies by default nor allies by obligation. They are other worlds.

### 2\. The revised core: care is powerful, not compulsory

The central conflict should no longer be peace versus war. It should be cooperation versus fear under real scarcity.

THE FOUR ACTIONS · PROVISIONAL RULES

LISTEN

Discover a district's current need and available reserves.

Gain understanding. Spend your turn.

SIGNAL

Request a voluntary conservation agreement using available reserves.

Potentially save resources across two rounds.

STRIKE

Forcibly interrupt extraction from a district for one round.

Fast and reliable, but damaging and potentially destabilizing.

HIDE

Conceal your station and reduce the risk of attack.

Preserve yourself; intervene nowhere.

Choose one of six district nodes first, then LISTEN, SIGNAL, or STRIKE. HIDE is always available as a global action. One action resolves per round.

### The indispensable distinction: need versus intent

A district extracting heavily might have enormous needs and no reserves. Another might have low needs and a large reserve.

Their visible extraction could look identical.

LISTEN reveals the confounder, rather than merely disclosing a more precise withdrawal figure. This makes the information strategically useful, but does not reveal a secret personality label.

Every round already provides passive observations:

* The previous round's exact basin flow and a broad withdrawal band for each district.

* All public signals and attacks, with attributed origins and destinations.

* Any visible local distress and whether an agreement was upheld.

There are no randomly unattributed attacks in version one. The first round includes one scripted historical snapshot.

In the initial model, baseline withdrawal depends on need, reserve pressure, and previous events—not directly on a district's hidden policy family. Policy affects how it responds to offers, threats, and relationships. This reduces the chance of identifying secret policies from extraction bands alone.

### Why cooperation should have the mechanical advantage

I propose the following illustrative balancing numbers, subject to simulation and playtesting.

| SIGNAL accepted | A district covers up to 4 units of its water need from stored reserves instead of current river extraction, for up to two rounds. Maximum 8 units saved.   |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SIGNAL refused  | No immediate water saving. The recipient communicates whether it lacks capacity or declines the agreement, without revealing its entire policy.            |
| STRIKE          | Prevent up to 5 units of extraction now, but destroy 2 units through disruption. The district may attempt up to 3 units of catch-up extraction next round. |
| HIDE            | No intervention, but lower exposure by 2.                                                                                                                  |

A successful agreement therefore has the potential to outperform an attack, while consuming a real, finite reserve. The district is choosing to spend part of its future resilience to protect the shared system.

An attack may improve downstream flow by as much as three units this round, but creates immediate waste and possible future extraction. It can also cause local hardship.

This closes the earlier perverse incentive: war cannot simply manufacture water by interrupting extraction without cost.

Example: Suppose the basin would receive 27 units, below its 30-unit minimum.

A successful SIGNAL could lift it to 31. A STRIKE could lift it to 30, narrowly preventing a shortfall—but may provoke retaliation and a future rebound.

If the district cannot afford to cooperate, SIGNAL may fail. In that emergency, an offensive move could be justified strategically.

That is the balance I want: compassion rewarded by causal effectiveness, not by an arbitrary morality meter.

### Make harm visible

The game should not treat the seventh community as the only one that matters.

If a strike deprives a district of water it genuinely needs, that hardship must be recorded and shown. Districts can suffer local distress, just as the downstream basin can.

A proposed failure rule is two consecutive rounds of serious unmet need for any community, including the seventh. Station destruction is a separate failure condition.

This prevents a strategy of preserving the downstream basin by systematically sacrificing the other six districts.

It also makes listening to a distressed district an act of practical strategic understanding rather than sentimental decoration.

Exposure starts at 0, is bounded from 0 to 4, and does not decay automatically. Exposure 2 or above makes the station targetable by an agent whose policy permits an attack.

Retaliation resolves in the same round as the initiating action. This means a round-six strike is not exempt from danger.

A strike also creates a persistent grievance record, influencing later cooperation. Exposure saturation does not erase that new grievance or eliminate the immediate risk of being targeted.

HIDE remains useful because hiding from exposure 4 only reduces it to 2; it does not necessarily restore complete safety.

Districts should also interact directly. For the first build, allow at most one autonomous district-to-district signal or strike per round. Its occurrence, sender, recipient, and consequences are public.

This is enough to create a living network without building a second full diplomacy simulator. The exact agent policies and event scheduling belong in the headless specification.

### The objective

A successful run completes all six resolved rounds with the station intact, the seventh basin viable, and no district suffering terminal deprivation.

A good ending is not necessarily a perfectly peaceful ending. A player may need to make a difficult protective intervention.

But indiscriminate aggression should usually produce a less resilient world.

That claim is a balancing intention, not an established outcome. We need to test whether the authored rules actually produce it.

### 4\. Visual lock: Indra's net in a dark universe

The five supplied references remain our visual authority. Their strongest synthesis is the luminous celestial network: countless fine connections whose local disturbances propagate through the larger whole.

THE DARK MICROCOSMOS

Six hidden worlds · One shared existence

Structural wireframe sketch, not final art. The production scene should retain the much finer filaments, dimensional obsidian terrain, celestial atmosphere, and intricate detail of your references.

The important visual decision is that a peaceful connection and a violent disruption both leave a trace in the same network.

Cooperation produces longer-lived luminous connections. A strike causes a sharp fracture and a visible interruption; the damage may repair, but the historical trace remains accessible.

Districts should use one reusable three-dimensional design with harmless variations independent of their private policies. No six separate miniature interiors in the first release.

The shared Maitrism family seal can remain the favicon if that convention is confirmed in the repository. It need not appear throughout the game itself. The game deserves its own identity within the larger canon.

### 5\. The final development sequence

* G0 — Provisional specification and hosting audit

    Document all observations, policy families, action costs, retaliation timing, resource accounting, local deprivation, and win/loss resolution. Inspect the Neurascape deployment before selecting the repository boundary.

    Gate: No undefined state transitions. No changes to existing routes.

* G1 — Headless simulation and adversarial tests

    Build a deterministic model with seeded scenarios and full event logs. Compare HIDE×6, random play, listening-first, cooperation-first, and reactive-strike policies. Add an omniscient benchmark and, for selected seeds, exhaustive or memoized search where computationally practical.

    Gate: Meaningful player leverage, verified resource accounting, and no universally dominant strategy across the tested scenarios.

* G1.5 — The ugly playable game

    Implement the whole interaction in flat 2D. Test with real people. Does LISTEN help them make better decisions? Can they explain why their choices mattered? Is six rounds enough?

    Gate: The game is intelligible, enjoyable, and strategically nontrivial. Only now freeze gameplay v1.0.

* G2 — Visual fidelity

    Implement the cosmic ring, signal filaments, black terrain, and four action effects. Compare actual renders to your five references. Establish named desktop and mobile test devices.

    Gate: Faithful visual language and measured performance, not merely attractive mockups.

* G3 — Integration and replay

    Connect the game engine to the visuals. Add the end-of-run reveal, accessible 2D fallback, reduced motion, and deterministic replays.

    Gate: Every animation reflects real simulated state; no hidden-policy leaks.

* G4 — Independent review and deployment

    Perform balancing, regression, accessibility, asset-rights, and performance reviews. Configure the production build for `/dark-microcosmos/`, verify the existing hosting architecture, and test the deployed site.

    Gate: A working, polished, independently playable canon addition.

The central numerical acceptance test is player leverage: across a seeded suite, how often can available player actions change the outcome compared with doing nothing?

The complementary test is informed agency: how much better can observation-limited strategies perform when they use LISTEN intelligently?

We should also compare outcomes across cooperative and aggressive strategies, while recording local harm. If perpetual striking wins most seeds, we have not implemented our intended game, regardless of how compassionate its prose sounds.

### 6\. The final design contract

THE DARK MICROCOSMOS · G0 CANDIDATE

### A compassionate dark forest

The fear is real. The interdependence is real. The choice is yours.

Six unknown districts draw from one living system. Their needs are hidden, their signals imperfect, and their histories entangled.

Listen when understanding matters. Signal when cooperation is possible. Strike when the danger warrants it. Hide when exposure threatens survival.

Discover whether a world can remain compassionate without becoming defenseless.

Even in the dark, no world exists alone.

如實 · 無為 · 道 · 愛 · 不二

My recommendation: accept this as the provisional G0 direction, not a prematurely frozen build specification. The implementation should begin by proving the mechanics and their incentives. The visual references remain a separate, equally demanding acceptance gate.

The result we are pursuing is not a game in which compassion always wins. It is a game in which compassion is strategically intelligent because other lives matter to one's own, while leaving genuine room for uncertainty, defense, tragedy, and discovery.

That is a compelling way to make Maitrādvaita playable without converting it into a sermon—and to honour the scientific restraint already visible in The Quiet Signal and The Seventh Seat.
