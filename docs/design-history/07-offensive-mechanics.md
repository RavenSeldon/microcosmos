# THE DARK MICROCOSMOS

BLACK DIALECTIC · OFFENSIVE MECHANICS · #PLAY

I love the underlying insight behind REVEAL, but I would not replace STRIKE with it yet.

The review has identified something more fundamental than an underpowered action: we're attacking the wrong thing.

Currently, STRIKE attacks a district, even when the player is trying to prevent a particular harmful action. The intervention creates damage, retaliation, and future resource costs that can outweigh whatever it prevents.

My preferred synthesis:

Make STRIKE a precise, consequential act of interdiction. Make REVEAL a potential way to use information offensively. Preserve both possibilities without adding buttons prematurely.

## 1. The black dialectic

| Candidate      | What excites me                                                                       | What concerns me                                                                       |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| REVEAL         | Makes hidden information strategically powerful; creates LISTEN → REVEAL combinations | Publishing information doesn't automatically change behaviour; it needs new rules      |
| SHIELD         | Sacrifice for another world is a brilliant compassionate mechanic                     | It removes the offensive option and risks becoming a straightforward resource exchange |
| JAM            | Fast, targeted, tactically useful; stops harm without reproducing it                  | Could become an automatic solution to every incoming threat                            |
| Refined STRIKE | Preserves aggression, uncertainty, and the satisfaction of decisive intervention      | Needs a credible cost and carefully defined consequences                               |

REVEAL is arguably the most original idea here, especially alongside the canon's interest in information becoming causally effective.

But its proposed consequences are not yet earned.

A district's legitimate need becoming public does not logically cancel a conditional attack triggered by heavy extraction. Likewise, exposing a district with large reserves does not automatically cause others to pressure it.

Those reactions require explicit mechanisms.

Otherwise we have merely replaced arbitrary violence with arbitrary social pressure.

## 2. My favourite solution: STRIKE the action, not the world

THE DARK MICROCOSMOS · INTERDICTION

E is preparing to attack B. You target E's ability to execute that action, severing its offensive connection for this round.

The threat is interrupted. Water is not automatically destroyed. Your station becomes exposed, and E remembers the interference.

This is essentially an evolved JAM mechanic, but I would retain the name STRIKE because it captures the significance and risk of the decision.

The proposed distinction is:

| Old STRIKE                                | Refined STRIKE                                              |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Disrupts water extraction                 | Disrupts a targeted network action                          |
| Destroys water automatically              | Prevents damage without creating equivalent physical damage |
| Creates catch-up extraction               | No artificial catch-up from interdiction alone              |
| Retaliation can be effectively inevitable | Retaliation remains possible and conditional                |
| Weak long-term value                      | Can prevent an attack and its downstream consequences       |

The action still carries costs: it exposes the station, establishes a grievance, and uses the only decision available that round.

And it can fail strategically. You might strike a district whose threat would have lapsed anyway, waste your intervention on the wrong actor, or provoke a subsequent attack on yourself.

Its power comes from changing what happens, rather than simply moving water around.

I would initially make it capable of cancelling a district's offensive network action when that action is actually scheduled. Don't add weapon upgrades, damage levels, or targeting precision sliders.

## 3. But REVEAL contains the more interesting long-term idea

The review's best contribution is not the new button. It is the proposed relationship:

Knowledge becomes leverage

LISTEN

Discover a fact

SIGNAL

Use the evidence

Possible future mechanic: A SIGNAL supported by recently verified evidence can influence a district differently from an unsupported request.

For example, a threatened district's verified scarcity might persuade a responsive aggressor to withdraw its threat. The causal effect must be implemented explicitly, not assumed.

That achieves much of REVEAL's purpose without requiring a fifth action or eliminating offensive force.

It also builds a natural two-turn strategy: discover something, then decide whether to use it.

There is a crucial ethical and strategic twist. Revealing information is not automatically compassionate. Publicly disclosing a vulnerable district's reserves might make it a more attractive target.

That makes information disclosure itself an interesting risk, entirely consistent with The Dark Microcosmos.

However, I would defer this evidence-backed SIGNAL mechanic until the 2D playtest establishes whether the existing information actions need help. It is an opportunity, not a settled rule.

## 4. What makes the STRIKE feel fun?

I think it needs three properties, not a complicated combat system.

Anticipation: You see a credible threat building between two worlds. You have just enough time to interpret it.

Commitment: STRIKE is decisive and visibly consequential, but you cannot undo the choice. Your one action is spent.

Aftermath: The severed filament fractures spectacularly. The attack fails to occur. Another district may react. The next round inherits the consequences.

Most importantly, the player should sometimes think, I shouldn't have struck, and sometimes, Thank goodness I did.

The game's replay can distinguish a prevented attack from a strike against an actor that would not have attacked. That is far more satisfying than displaying an arbitrary violence penalty.

## 5. The smallest experiment I would authorize

Yes, run the comparison—but do not replace STRIKE on conceptual appeal alone.

Use the existing baseline and matched seeds, with the following staged comparison:

1. JAM / precise interdiction first. Remove extraction damage and catch-up, retain targeted network interruption, then measure actual prevented attacks, station retaliation, district harm, and wins.
2. SHIELD second. Test absorbing an incoming attack as a separate candidate, particularly whether sacrificing integrity ever produces superior shared outcomes.
3. REVEAL third. Before measuring its effectiveness, specify exactly what becomes public, who responds, when they respond, and whether exposure of a vulnerable district can cause harm. Treat these response rules as new assumptions.
4. Compare information-limited agents. Teach bots to interpret incoming threats. Measure whether interventions improve outcomes over a six-round horizon and whether a two-action plan ever beats an immediate SIGNAL.

Do not conflate a mechanic's success rate with its gameplay quality. An action that is rarely used can still be excellent if its rare use produces a memorable, understandable decision.

And don't tune the distribution simply to force every action into equal usage.

### The checkpoint I care about

After this comparison, build the 2D version. We are approaching diminishing returns from simulations without human feedback.

I would rather have a rough, playable version with one exciting offensive intervention than a mathematically elaborate system in which every decision is accounted for but nothing feels alive.

## My design verdict

PROVISIONAL DESIGN LOCK

# Strike the threat. Not the world.

LISTEN to understand.

SIGNAL to cooperate.

STRIKE to interrupt harm.

HIDE to survive.

The forest stays dangerous. The player remains vulnerable. Cooperation is usually advantageous, but force retains a legitimate, consequential role.

Even in darkness, restraint can be an act of strength.

My preference is refined STRIKE as the default candidate, with REVEAL retained as the strongest future extension. That preserves the original promise of a compassionate dark forest without making the game pacifist or introducing an unnecessary fifth action.

The philosophical question also becomes richer: not merely whether the player can destroy another district, but whether they can recognize the difference between stopping harm and reproducing it.

That's a game worth making.
