# The Dark Microcosmos — G1a: Harm and Terminal Accounting

Run of 14 November 2026 · baseline rules unchanged · companion to `RESULTS.md`, which is untouched

## Read This First

- **No rule changed.** G1a adds a ledger and a fixed six-round evaluation on top of the v0.3
  baseline. Every game plays out exactly as before; the baseline outputs regenerate byte for byte.
- **The win or loss shown here is the legacy outcome** under the unchanged rules. No new failure
  threshold was introduced.
- **Fixed horizon.** When a game ends early, the world runs on to round six with the station inert.
  Every figure below is per world over the same six rounds, so policies can now be compared.
- **"Against doing nothing"** means the paired difference from an inert station in the same world,
  with a 95% interval. It is what a policy's own actions added or removed.
- **Two seed suites.** Development seeds 1–500 (1–150 chose the river calibration). Held-out seeds
  100001–100500, never run before this. Having been run, they are no longer untouched; later gates
  need a fresh range.
- **The policies are the same ten simple bots.** They show what simple play does, not what is best.

## Summary

1. **All six acceptance criteria are met** (§1).
2. **Cooperation is a trade, not a free gain.** Against doing nothing, the plain cooperative policy
   cuts basin harm by 3.50 units and costs the districts 6.25 units of stored water, 1.40 units of
   extra unmet need, and 0.45 more districts ending with nothing stored.
3. **Striking is worse than doing nothing on every measure, the basin included.** Basin harm rises
   by 1.08 to 1.82 units, district hardship by 1.62 to 3.83, water destroyed by 3.03 to 5.74.
4. **A legacy win hides a great deal.** Of the plain cooperative policy's wins, 87.2% end with at
   least one district holding nothing and 59.9% include unmet need. The average win spends 14.29 of
   the network's 32.49 units of stored water.
5. **The network fights without the player.** With the station inert there are 4.30
   district-on-district strikes per world, destroying 8.61 units of water.
6. **The G1 results hold on held-out seeds, with one revision:** an opening LISTEN shows a small
   gain for one of the two policies tested (§5).

## 1. Acceptance Criteria

| Criterion from the Decision Record | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Every unit of water is accounted for, including reserve transfers and destruction | Test `water: ...`: 3,000 random games, inert rounds included. River in = taken + destroyed + flow. Per district: need = river use + agreement draw + emergency draw + unmet; reserve after = before − draws + stored. |
| The cumulative ledger equals the sum of the per-round records | Test `ledger: ...`: 1,000 games, every total re-derived independently. |
| Every policy has a comparable six-round window | Test `window: ...`: all ten bots × 300 worlds are evaluated over exactly six rounds; inert rounds are exactly those after the game ended. |
| Final-round agreements and strikes preserve their outstanding obligations | Three hand-computed tests `obligations: ...`. |
| Original seeds remain reproducible | A golden record frozen before any G1a code was written; tests `legacy: ...` compare against it. All nine baseline outputs regenerated byte for byte after the change. |
| Baseline rules and results remain available unchanged | `RESULTS.md` and `out/<config>.json` are untouched. G1a writes only `out/g1a-*.json`, `out/heldout-baseline.json` and this file. |

On the caution about memoisation: the ledger is derived from the round records and is not part of
the game state, so the search and its keys are unaffected. If a later rule makes hardship terminal,
it must enter the state and the key at that point.

Suite: 37 tests pass (23 baseline, 14 new).

## 2. What the Ledger Records

| Quantity | Definition as implemented |
|---|---|
| District hardship | Unmet need per district, after any reserve use, summed over six rounds. |
| Basin harm | Sum of the downstream deficits below the minimum of 30 over six rounds. |
| Reserve depletion | Reserve entering round one and after round six; draws under agreements; draws in emergencies; water stored. |
| Water destruction | Units lost to strikes, by the station or by districts. |
| Unresolved obligations | Catch-up extraction still owed; agreement rounds still running; the most reserve those rounds can still draw. |
| Station harm | Integrity lost; final integrity and exposure. |

**Using a reserve is recorded separately from harm.** A draw under an agreement is a chosen use of
storage. A draw in an emergency is forced by a shortfall. Neither counts as hardship; only unmet
need does.

Choices I had to make, each tagged **[A]** in `SPEC.md` §8:

- "Essential need" is not defined in the record, so all need counts as essential.
- An inert station keeps its exposure and can still be hit until its integrity reaches zero.
- "Initial reserve" is the reserve entering round one, after the scripted history round.

## 3. What Each Kind of Policy Does to the World

Paired against doing nothing, development seeds, 95% intervals. Held-out figures are in Table B.

| | District hardship | Basin harm | Stored water | Districts ending empty |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Doing nothing (absolute level) | 2.66 | 12.33 | −1.58 | 0.43 |
| Cooperate, hide when exposed | +1.40 (+1.13 to +1.67) | −3.50 (−3.84 to −3.15) | −6.25 (−6.86 to −5.64) | +0.45 (+0.39 to +0.51) |
| Informed mixed, no listen | +1.76 (+1.45 to +2.07) | −4.20 (−4.53 to −3.87) | −7.42 (−8.03 to −6.81) | +0.50 (+0.44 to +0.57) |
| Alternate strike | +1.62 (+1.46 to +1.77) | +1.08 (+0.86 to +1.30) | −1.13 (−1.30 to −0.96) | +0.17 (+0.13 to +0.21) |
| Strike every round | +3.83 (+3.59 to +4.07) | +1.52 (+1.25 to +1.78) | −1.30 (−1.48 to −1.13) | +0.10 (+0.06 to +0.15) |

- **The exchange rate.** The plain cooperative policy spends about 1.8 units of stored water for
  each unit of basin harm avoided (6.25 ÷ 3.50; held-out 6.89 ÷ 3.71). Water saved in a round that
  was not short prevents no harm, and refilling afterwards takes some of it back.
- **Why cooperation raises hardship.** Agreements drain reserves, and a drained district has less
  to fall back on when another district strikes it. I checked this by switching district events
  off: cooperation then adds 0.00 hardship (`out/g1a-hub-check.json`). All of the extra hardship
  comes through the network.
- **Strikes do not help the basin over six rounds.** A strike is worth +3 now and costs about 4 the
  next round in catch-up and refill (hand-computed test in `test/engine.test.ts`). G1's finding
  that strikes shift water in time stands; the ledger shows the shift is a net loss.
- **Cooperation also reduces destruction**, by 1.66 units for the plain policy. District-on-district
  strikes fall from 4.30 to 3.49 per world. Two things could cause that and I have not separated
  them: districts under agreement extract less, and a relayed offer uses the round's one network slot.

## 4. What a Legacy Win Contains

For "cooperate, hide when exposed" (187 wins on development seeds, 200 held-out):

| Among its wins | Development | Held-out |
|---|---:|---:|
| At least one district ends with nothing stored | 87.2% | 80.5% |
| Some need went unmet | 59.9% | 56.0% |
| Obligations still owed after round six | 70.6% | 69.0% |
| Mean change in stored water | −14.29 | −14.48 |
| Mean basin harm | 4.70 | 4.45 |
| Mean district hardship | 3.40 | 2.92 |

The network enters round one holding 32.49 units on average, so a typical cooperative win spends
about 44% of it. The current win condition sees none of this.

Outstanding obligations are small in size. Across policies, agreements still running after round
six can draw at most 0.54 units per world on average, and 1.5 to 2.5 units of catch-up extraction
are owed, most of it from district-on-district strikes (Table C). The round-six loophole is real
and minor for these bots.

## 6. What the Network Does on Its Own

With the station inert for six rounds (development seeds; held-out in brackets):

| | Per world |
|---|---:|
| District-on-district strikes | 4.30 (4.09) |
| Worlds with no such strike | 23.2% (24.6%) |
| Water destroyed | 8.61 (8.18) |
| District hardship | 2.66 (2.37) |
| Basin harm | 12.33 (12.30) |

This level of conflict comes from the Preemptive rule I assumed in G1: such a district strikes the
heaviest extractor in the high band. It was not visible in G1 because losing games stopped early.
It matters for the later gates. There is already plenty for a protective STRIKE to interdict, and
a district that cooperates becomes easier to hurt.

## 7. What This Hands Back to You

G1a authorised accounting only. These follow from it and are not decided here.

1. **Is cooperation better?** Under the legacy win rule, yes. Under the ledger it trades basin harm
   for district reserves and some district hardship. Ranking the two requires weights, and choosing
   them is a value judgement: how much district resilience is one unit of basin harm worth? I have
   not chosen any.
2. **Should a win look at districts at all?** Today 87.2% of cooperative wins leave a district empty.
3. **Is the network's own violence intended?** It is my assumption, and it drives the hardship.
4. **Basin thresholds for G1b** can now be set against real distributions: mean basin harm is 12.33
   with an inert station and about 8 to 9 under most of the cooperative policies.

## 8. What This Cannot Tell You

- What the best policy does. The ledger is reported for ten simple bots, not for optimal play.
- Whether any of these costs would be visible or meaningful to a player.
- Anything beyond six rounds. Obligations are recorded, not simulated.
